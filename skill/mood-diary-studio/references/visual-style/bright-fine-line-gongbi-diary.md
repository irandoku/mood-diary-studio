# Bright Fine-Line Gongbi Diary

Use this as the default, portable rendering treatment for DIARY results unless
a more specific approved or current-entry treatment overrides it:

> 明透細線工筆淡彩手帳風 — bright, airy, fine-line, gongbi-like,
> transparent light-watercolor mood diary.

This is visual grammar, not character canon. Approved character identity,
exact character ID, reference authority, and character-specific restrictions
remain higher priority. A current DIARY request changes the effective entry
style only; it does not revise the stored card policy.

## Core rule

> Let linework describe; let pale color breathe.

The page should feel like a mood-diary fragment gradually appearing on paper,
not a finished commercial illustration placed on top of a background.

## Paper and negative space

- Prefer cool-white or near-white paper.
- Let the paper remain an active compositional area, not a neutral backdrop.
- Keep generous breathing space and visibly incomplete or unpainted areas.
- Use quiet continuation of the same room, sky, floor, or window light when
  the breathing area needs context; blankness is not required.
- Do not apply an overall beige, sepia, brown-yellow, or nostalgic wash.

## Linework

- Use very fine warm gray-brown, pale ink-brown, or another character-fit
  colored line.
- Keep structural lines precise, controlled, and locally fine-detailed.
- Allow a small amount of dry-brush, broken, or open-ended line where it helps
  the paper feeling; do not turn hand-drawn into sloppy construction.
- Preserve important silhouette, proportion, limb, marking, and object
  structure even when the treatment is delicate.
- Avoid uniform thick black outlines.

## Color and shadow

- Use transparent, low-muddiness, low-saturation watercolor washes.
- Let color support the linework instead of replacing all volume with opaque
  blocks.
- For white or pale characters, use paper white with only pale blue-gray or
  neutral-gray separation where needed.
- Reserve brown for character features, wood, and necessary local materials;
  do not tint the whole page brown.
- Keep shadows extremely light, preferring neutral gray or pale blue-gray.

## Detail hierarchy

Use this hierarchy:

> Important areas receive controlled detail; secondary areas are lightly
> described; atmospheric areas may remain unpainted.

Concentrate precision on identity anchors, face and expression, the core prop,
the cultural or narrative carrier, and a small amount of foreground texture.
Do not distribute equal detail across every corner.

## Diary grammar

The result should read as:

- one mood-diary page fragment;
- a page that could carry an authorized date and one short sentence;
- a pause before or after an event;
- restrained, low-amplitude, lived-in emotion.

Avoid commercial key art, product display, tourism promotion, information
poster, sticker sheet, or complete animation-background treatment.

## Scene scale

For a short chibi mascot, prefer a low-height relationship:

- tatami, a room threshold, engawa, or a window-side floor seat;
- cushion, low shelf, small wooden table, or floor-level objects;
- readable full body and limb geometry;
- support contact and occlusion explained by the action.

Avoid human-height tables or chairs, sitting on a tabletop, lower-body loss
behind a surface, proportion drift caused by furniture, or unapproved
anthropomorphic clothing and poses. The character card decides whether any
exception is allowed.

For a low-density daily scene, start with three to five meaningful props or
cues only when they support the emotional center. A moderate reading corner
may add a low shelf, a small stack of books, a card, basket, vase, or plant,
but the background must retain breathing room.

## Consistency references

A light three-view reference (front, side, and back) may help keep silhouette,
proportion, ear or appendage direction, tail placement, and major marking
locations stable across scenes. Treat it as a consistency aid only, not as a
strict animation turnaround or new canonical data. The approved character card
and authorized references remain authoritative; unresolved or occluded views
stay unknown.

## Weather and special topics

For rain or overcast weather, use localized evidence of atmosphere:

- fine rain beyond a window;
- damp foliage;
- gray-blue distance;
- a pale blue-gray local shadow;
- cool-white paper retained indoors.

Do not cover the whole frame with a gray filter or turn a quiet cool mood into
dramatic sadness.

For commemorative or cultural topics, a layered profile is allowed when the
environment carries the narrative. Keep one action, one complete scene, a
detail zone, and a quieter breathing zone. Use culturally specific clothing,
patterns, ceremonies, symbols, and labels only with user-provided or reliable
authorized provenance. Do not merge unrelated motifs into generic decoration.

## Low-amplitude emotion vocabulary

Use small changes in eyes, gaze, mouth, head angle, body tension, and prop
handling. Mascot readability matters more than human-level facial anatomy.

| Emotion | Direction |
|---|---|
| Calm reading | Natural half-lidded eyes, steady book hold, relaxed body. |
| Faint emptiness | Gaze slightly unfocused, attention drifting, neutral mouth. |
| Mild fatigue | Heavier lids, slight downward posture, prop held lower. |
| Small happiness | Barely lifted mouth corners and gentle eyes; no broad laugh. |
| Thoughtful pause | Gaze to the side or upward, reading interrupted by thought. |
| Wordless ennui | More visible half-lidded eyes and quiet non-reaction; not anger. |

Do not add sweat drops, anger marks, giant tears, explosive mouths, or other
reaction symbols unless explicitly requested and narratively supported.

## Text placement

If text is authorized, keep the date, short phrase, or special-topic title
brief and in a quiet paper area. A left-text/right-scene arrangement is valid
for a special topic, but text must remain subordinate and must not turn the
page into a poster. Without authorization, add no text.

## Common failure checks

Before delivery, confirm that the selected treatment does not produce:

- thick black contour lines;
- opaque or muddy watercolor that hides structure;
- full-frame sepia, yellow, or gray grading;
- dense decoration in every corner;
- exaggerated sticker-like facial acting;
- glossy, plastic, 3D, or thick-painted rendering;
- genericized character markings;
- unapproved anthropomorphism;
- a commercial poster, catalog, or background showcase.
