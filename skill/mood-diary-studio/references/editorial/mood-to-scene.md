# Mood-to-Scene Translation

Translate emotion through spatial and behavioral choices, not facial expression alone.

## Decision dimensions

### Posture

- withdrawal: compact posture, softened shoulders, reduced reach;
- anticipation: slight forward lean, gaze toward open space;
- fatigue: supported weight, lowered energy, incomplete action;
- relief: released posture, settled object placement;
- quiet joy: small outward movement, relaxed spacing;
- uncertainty: paused gesture, uneven distance from the next action.

Avoid theatrical body language unless the source clearly supports it.

### Space

- isolation: greater empty area and separated objects;
- possibility: open space ahead of the gaze;
- pressure: reduced breathing room without filling every surface;
- lingering thought: a faint echo near the edge rather than a second full scene;
- recovery: stable horizontal composition and fewer visual demands.

### Motion

Use one restrained motion cue when helpful: a curtain edge, loose page, ribbon, drifting steam, shadow direction, or weather trace. Do not stack motion symbols.

### Object relationships

Prefer distance, orientation, and interruption over extra props. An untouched cup placed beyond reach may communicate more than several literal emotion icons.

## Symbolic elements

Use at most one symbolic element under the default visual budget. Keep it subordinate to the lived scene. Reject symbols that merely label the emotion, such as a large broken heart for disappointment or a trophy for relief.

## Selection test

Keep a visual choice only when removing it would weaken the emotional relationship. Otherwise omit it.
