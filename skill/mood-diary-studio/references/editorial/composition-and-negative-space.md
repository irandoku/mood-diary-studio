# Composition and Negative Space

Negative space is spatial rhythm, visual direction, pause, or a quiet text
area. It may contain a low-detail continuation of the environment and need not
be a blank or single-color background.

## Plan the frame

Decide:

- character scale: small, medium, or close;
- character zone;
- gaze or motion direction;
- detail-concentration zone;
- breathing zone;
- supporting-object zone;
- optional text zone.

Prefer small or medium character scale for reflective entries. Use close scale
only when a small gesture or identity detail is the emotional event.

Concentrate high contrast, sharper edges, texture, and object specificity in
one detail zone. Keep the breathing zone quieter through lower contrast,
lighter detail, greater spacing, or continuous atmosphere rather than
automatically erasing the setting.

## Narrative functions

Assign at least one function to the breathing zone:

- express absence or emotional distance;
- open a direction for expectation;
- let a pause remain unresolved;
- separate the character from an object or threshold;
- reduce cognitive pressure;
- reserve quiet placement for approved text.

A breathing zone may include pale sky, a quiet wall or floor plane, soft
shadow, distant foliage, a curtain, or another low-salience scene continuation
when it supports the same function.

If the zone has no function, reconsider the composition rather than
automatically filling or blanking it.

## Scene coherence

Use one complete, focused scene. Let every environmental cue support the same
emotional center and spatial relationship. Under `moderate`, 2-4 cues may form
a coherent setting, but they must not compete with the character or become a
background showcase.

## Avoid

- centering every element by default;
- placing decoration in all corners;
- enlarging the character until no breathing room remains;
- distributing equal detail across the whole panorama;
- using a pure blank background only to satisfy a low-density label;
- letting the date or phrase dominate the emotional scene;
- treating blankness as a style adjective without defining its role.

## Prompt expression

State breathing space positively and spatially:

```text
Keep the right half as a quieter, lower-detail breathing area, with pale sky
and soft window shadow continuing the scene because the character is looking
toward an outing that has not happened yet.
```

Avoid vague phrases such as `lots of whitespace` without narrative purpose.
