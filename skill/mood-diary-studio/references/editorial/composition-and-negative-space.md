# Composition and Negative Space

Negative space must carry narrative or reading function.

## Plan the frame

Decide:

- character scale: small, medium, or close;
- character zone;
- gaze or motion direction;
- primary empty zone;
- supporting-object zone;
- optional text zone.

Prefer small or medium character scale for reflective entries. Use close scale only when a small gesture or identity detail is the emotional event.

## Narrative functions

Assign at least one function to the primary empty zone:

- express absence or emotional distance;
- open a direction for expectation;
- let a pause remain unresolved;
- separate the character from an object or threshold;
- reduce cognitive pressure;
- reserve quiet placement for approved text.

If the empty area has no function, reconsider the composition rather than filling it.

## Avoid

- centering every element by default;
- placing decoration in all corners;
- enlarging the character until no breathing room remains;
- using a detailed panorama when one cue establishes the setting;
- letting the date or phrase dominate the emotional scene;
- treating blankness as a style adjective without defining its role.

## Prompt expression

State negative space positively and spatially:

```text
Keep the right half calm and mostly open because the character is looking toward
an outing that has not happened yet.
```

Avoid vague phrases such as `lots of whitespace` without narrative purpose.
